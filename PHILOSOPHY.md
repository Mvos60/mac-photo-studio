# Mac Photo Studio Philosophy

## Why Mac Photo Studio Exists

Mac Photo Studio exists to protect photographs.

It is not intended to replace a Digital Asset Manager or a RAW editor.
digiKam and darktable already perform those jobs well.

Mac Photo Studio provides the trust layer between the camera and the
photographer's archive. It helps photographs enter that archive safely,
verifiably and with a traceable history.

## Core Principles

### Protect originals

Original camera files are never modified automatically.

Camera media remains read-only throughout the import process. The photographer
decides when cards are formatted.

### Observe before acting

Mac Photo Studio discovers and verifies first.

Only after presenting the facts does it ask the photographer to make a
decision.

### Trust through verification

Important operations should be verified.

Copies are verified. Imports are verified. Provenance evidence is verified.

Trust is earned through evidence rather than assumption.

### Recover before deleting

Potentially destructive actions should be recoverable.

Where practical, photographs and provenance evidence are quarantined before
permanent removal.

### Record the photographic story

Significant events in a photograph's life may become part of its provenance:

- import
- edit
- derivative creation
- export
- verification
- recovery

The goal is not surveillance. The goal is preserving photographic history.

### Work with existing tools

Mac Photo Studio complements existing photography software:

- digiKam manages catalogues, metadata, ratings, searches and faces.
- darktable develops RAW photographs and creates exports.
- Mac Photo Studio protects and verifies the workflow connecting them.

### Keep the photographer in control

The software should explain what it is doing.

It should never surprise the photographer.

The final decision belongs to the person behind the camera.

## Long-Term Vision

Mac Photo Studio should become a trusted companion for photographers.

Its success is measured not by its number of features, but by the confidence
that original photographs are protected and the workflow remains trustworthy.

If the software quietly does its job while the photographer concentrates on
making photographs, it has succeeded.
